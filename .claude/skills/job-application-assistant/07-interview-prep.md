## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. bhangrascape.ca — End-to-End Platform Delivery (Ownership / Full-Stack Engineering)
**S:** A 20+ member bhangra performance group had no centralized way to manage events, media, or member coordination — everything was done over WhatsApp and spreadsheets.
**T:** Design, build, and deploy a production-grade event management platform solo, from zero, covering frontend, backend, auth, cloud infrastructure, and CI/CD.
**A:** Built a Next.js frontend + Node.js/Express API + PostgreSQL/Prisma backend. Implemented JWT, Google OAuth, RBAC, and Zod validation for security. Architected S3 presigned URL media ingestion to keep files off the API server. Provisioned all cloud infrastructure with Terraform (Azure + AWS). Containerized with Docker/Kubernetes and set up CI/CD on Vercel and Render. Owned every layer from design to production monitoring.
**R:** Live production platform serving the group. Proved ability to own a full product lifecycle — infra, auth, data layer, CI/CD — without a team.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a project you're proud of," "Describe a time you worked independently," "What have you built end-to-end?"

### 2. Amazon — SLA Performance & Runbook Authoring (Reliability / Documentation)
**S:** As a Technical Support Associate at Amazon, the team handled 1,000+ complex technical and operational issues under strict SLA timelines. New hires had long onboarding ramps because tribal knowledge was undocumented.
**T:** Maintain personal SLA resolution rate while also improving team knowledge transfer.
**A:** Diagnosed and resolved complex issues at root cause rather than symptom level, consistently meeting SLAs. Identified recurring escalation patterns and authored SOPs and runbooks covering common failure modes. Shared documentation proactively with the team.
**R:** Maintained a 95% SLA resolution rate across 1,000+ cases. Runbooks and SOPs were adopted by 10+ new hires and cut onboarding time by 20%.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you improved a process," "Give an example of handling high-volume work under pressure," "Describe your approach to documentation."

### 3. Terraform/Azure Blog Series — Teaching Complex Topics (Communication / Self-Direction)
**S:** While completing my Advanced Diploma, I was learning Terraform and Azure infrastructure in parallel with coursework. There was very little beginner-friendly content that showed full real-world IaC patterns (not just hello-world examples).
**T:** Consolidate my own learning AND create accessible reference material for others going through the same Azure infrastructure learning curve.
**A:** Published 19 technical blog posts at ekamblogs.ca covering progressively more complex Azure patterns: App Service, PostgreSQL Flexible Server, VNet, AKS, ACR, OIDC CI/CD, Azure Policy, remote state — plus Jenkins CI/CD with SonarQube/Trivy, Docker/Kubernetes with Prometheus/Grafana monitoring, and n8n automation.
**R:** 19 posts published. The blog demonstrates production-level depth on Azure/DevOps tooling and serves as a portfolio of practical infrastructure knowledge beyond what any diploma curriculum covers.
**Use for:** "What do you do outside of work to grow technically?" "Give an example of self-directed learning," "How do you communicate complex technical topics?"

### 4. The Beer Store Co-op — Process Improvement Under Constraints (Efficiency / Attention to Detail)
**S:** During a warehouse co-op, cross-dock processing was slower than it needed to be, and inventory accuracy was a persistent operational concern.
**T:** Contribute meaningfully to warehouse operations in a short 4-month term while maintaining accuracy standards.
**A:** Applied systematic attention to inventory tracking processes. Identified inefficiencies in cross-dock sequencing and proposed streamlined procedures. Maintained meticulous record-keeping throughout.
**R:** Achieved 98% inventory accuracy (above team baseline). Cross-dock processing time reduced by 15%.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you improved efficiency," "Give an example of detail-orientation," "Describe working in a fast-paced operational environment."

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why did you leave [previous company]?"
> Amazon was a formative experience — I learned how high-reliability systems work and how to communicate under pressure. I left to return to Canada and pursue software development formally. My Amazon experience actually sharpened my appreciation for system reliability and documentation, which I've brought into my cloud/DevOps work.

### "You don't have [specific skill/experience] — you only have a diploma / limited professional experience."
> That's fair, and I'll be honest about where I am. What I can point to is a production platform I built and deployed solo — full-stack, cloud infrastructure, CI/CD, auth — and 19 technical blog posts that show I can go deep on complex topics. I graduated Magna Cum Laude and earned my Azure cert while doing both. I don't have 3 years of professional dev experience, but I operate above that experience level on my primary stack, and I have the demonstrated self-direction to close gaps fast.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> In 5 years, I want to be a mid-to-senior developer who has shipped real product features and owns meaningful parts of the system. I'm drawn to the cloud/platform side — ideally I'm leading smaller infrastructure decisions and mentoring junior engineers by then. But right now I'm focused on the first 2 years: learning from a real team, shipping production code, and getting my AZ-104 and AZ-400 certs.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> Professional experience depth — I haven't had a full-time developer role yet (my dev work has been freelance and college projects). I'm aware of that gap and I've tried to compensate by going deep: building a live production platform, writing 19 technical blog posts to force real understanding, and earning my Azure cert. I'm a fast learner and I know what I don't know.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
